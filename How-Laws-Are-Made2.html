How Laws Are Made

Interactive Lesson - Year 7 - MSC Studies

What rules help keep our school safe?
Answer:
Rules like walking in corridors, no fighting, listening to teachers, following safety drills, and respecting others - they keep everyone safe and help learning happen.
What would happen if there were no laws?
Answer:
Without laws there would be chaos: people could harm each other without consequences, no one would feel safe, and society would not function. Laws protect rights, keep order, and give fair consequences.
Should there be consequences? Who decides the punishment? Why do societies need laws?
Answer:
Yes - stealing harms others and breaks trust. The court (judicial branch) decides the punishment based on the law. Societies need laws to protect people, ensure fairness, and keep order.
Mini Check: Is a school rule a law? Why or why not?
Answer:
No - a school rule is not a law. Laws are made by the government and apply to everyone in society, with official consequences. School rules apply only inside the school and are enforced by the school.
Mini Check: What happens to a bill after it is proposed?
Answer:
After a bill is proposed, it is debated, then voted on. If approved, it becomes an official law that must be followed.
Mini Check: Who elects members of the legislative branch in the UAE?
Answer:
Members of the Federal National Council (FNC) are partly elected by Emirati citizens and partly appointed by the rulers of each emirate.
Mini Check: Name a group in the executive branch.
Answer:
The police, the Ministry of Interior, and other government ministries are part of the executive branch - they enforce and carry out the laws.
Mini Check: Who decides if someone is innocent or guilty?
Answer:
Judges in the courts (the judicial branch) decide if someone is innocent or guilty, based on evidence and the law.
Mini Check: Is a traffic fine civil or criminal law?
Answer:
A traffic fine is usually criminal law - it is the state punishing someone for breaking traffic rules. (However, if the traffic accident causes damage and one driver sues another for compensation, that part is civil law.)
Mini Check: What is a criminal law example in UAE?
Answer:
Examples: theft, cybercrime, assault, drug offences, traffic violations like reckless driving. These are crimes against society and are prosecuted by the state.
Mini Check: Who listens to evidence in a trial?
Answer:
The judge listens to evidence in a trial. In some countries a jury also listens and decides on guilt or innocence. In the UAE, judges (not juries) usually decide cases.
Mini Check: Why is debating important in law-making?
Answer:
Debating helps lawmakers hear different viewpoints, consider pros and cons, and improve the law before it is passed. It makes laws fairer and stronger because many sides are considered.
Mini Check: Who enforces laws in your community?
Answer:
The police enforce laws in the community. Other authorities like traffic police, customs officers, and government inspectors also help enforce specific laws.
Match each description: Creates laws / Enforces laws / Decides legal cases
Answer:
Creates laws -> Legislative branch (Federal National Council in the UAE)
Enforces laws -> Executive branch (police, Ministry of Interior)
Decides legal cases -> Judicial branch (the courts)
Decide if each is Parliamentary or Presidential, and which fits the UAE.
Answer:
1. Leader elected directly by people, head of state -> Presidential system.
2. Prime Minister chosen from the party with most seats in parliament -> Parliamentary system.
3. UAE has a Federal National Council that advises the government -> UAE uses a federal system with a consultative council, combining elements of both systems.
Classify each: traffic fine, neighbour property dispute, cybercrime hacking, broken business contract, shoplifting.
Answer:
1. Traffic violation fine -> Criminal Law (breaking state rules).
2. Neighbours dispute property boundaries -> Civil Law (dispute between individuals).
3. Cybercrime by hacking accounts -> Criminal Law (crime against society).
4. Business breaks a contract agreement -> Civil Law (disagreement between parties).
5. Person caught shoplifting -> Criminal Law (offence against the state).
Put these law-making steps in the correct order (1-5): Law enforced / Bill drafted / Courts interpret / Bill debated / Bill approved
Answer:
1. Bill is drafted and proposed
2. Bill is debated in parliament
3. Bill is approved and becomes law
4. Law is enforced by police and authorities
5. Courts and judges interpret the law

Discuss the pros and cons of each bill. Who benefits? Who might be harmed? Is it fair?
Answer:
Bill 1 - Mark students absent if late more than 5 minutes: Pros: encourages punctuality. Cons: unfair to students who face traffic or family issues.

Bill 2 - Pass/Fail grading instead of percentages: Pros: less stress. Cons: hides effort differences, may hurt university applications.

Bill 3 - Ban group work for all assignments: Pros: stops free riders. Cons: students miss teamwork and communication skills.
Which branch should handle a cybercrime case? Why?
Answer:
All three branches play a role:
- Legislative creates the cyber-crime laws.
- Executive (police) investigate and arrest suspects.
- Judicial (courts) decide guilt and sentence.
The judicial branch handles the actual case in court.
Is a traffic fine civil or criminal law? Explain.
Answer:
A traffic fine is criminal law because the driver has broken a rule made by the state, and the state issues the punishment.
What could happen if cyber-safety laws did not exist in the UAE?
Answer:
Without cyber-safety laws, hackers could steal personal information, money, and identities with no consequences. People would not trust online banking. Children would be exposed to harmful content. Businesses would suffer cyber-attacks.
Should students debate school rules? Why or why not?
Answer:
Yes - debating school rules helps students think about fairness, learn how laws are made, and feel respected. The final decision still rests with the school leadership.
How do laws protect you in your daily life?
Answer:
Laws protect you every day: traffic laws keep roads safe, cyber-safety laws protect your data online, criminal laws protect you from theft, school and labour laws protect your right to education, and consumer laws protect you when you shop.
Which branch creates laws? (Legislative / Executive / Judicial)
Answer:
Legislative branch - in the UAE, the Federal National Council drafts and proposes laws.
True or False: Civil law deals with crimes against society.
Answer:
False. Civil law deals with disputes between individuals or organisations. Criminal law deals with crimes against society.
Scenario: A students phone is stolen - civil or criminal?
Answer:
Criminal law - theft is a crime against the state.
After a bill is drafted, what happens next?
Answer:
The bill is debated by the legislative body (in the UAE, the Federal National Council).
Name one role of the judicial branch.
Answer:
The judicial branch interprets laws, decides legal cases, judges guilt or innocence, and ensures justice is delivered fairly.
