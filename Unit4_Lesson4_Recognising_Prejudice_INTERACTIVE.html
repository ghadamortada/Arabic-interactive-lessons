😠 Why do you think people can sometimes be mean to each other?
Why do some people have prejudices against other countries?

Often, people are mean or prejudiced because of fear of the unknown. When we don't know someone, we make assumptions — and those assumptions can lead to unfair judgements. Today, we'll explore what prejudice really is, where it comes from, and how we can overcome it.

🔍

Define prejudice and explain where it comes from

📖

Analyse two stories showing prejudice in action

🗳️

Participate in a walking debate on statements about prejudice

💪

Suggest ways to challenge and overcome prejudice

Activity 2 — Where Does Prejudice Come From?
4 / 13
🎤
Teacher says

"Let's go back in time to understand WHY prejudice exists. Read this article carefully — it explains the roots of fear and prejudice."

Long ago, people used to be afraid of others who wore different clothes, had different facial features, or who did things in a different way. Long ago, there were no cars or aeroplanes either. That meant that people didn't have the means of transport to travel and meet different people. They usually stayed in the place where they were born, with the same people they had grown up with and never saw an unfamiliar face or tried unfamiliar food.

It's no wonder then that people were afraid when they saw strangers from neighbouring communities. They probably thought they were there to rob them! Maybe we have even heard stories that have been passed down through the generations about 'bad' people from faraway places.

Nowadays, we all have the freedom and the ability to visit or live in different places. Therefore, we are constantly meeting new people from different backgrounds. Not only are we meeting different people in real life, but we are also getting to know the world through television and social media. Documentaries and TV shows enable us to learn about other cultures and social media provides us with even more information.

Never before have people from all over the world been so close. We live and work side by side, so we need to respect each other's differences and overcome any prejudices that we have.
💭

Why were people afraid of strangers long ago? Does that same fear still exist today — even without the same reasons?

TV documentaries, films, news and social media have exposed people to cultures from around the world without needing to travel. However, media can also reinforce prejudice if it only shows negative stereotypes.

Activity 3 — Jack's Story
6 / 13
🎤
Teacher says

"Read this story carefully. Think about how a prejudice — formed from someone else's opinion — was shattered by actually getting to know the person."

Last year, Jack joined our class. His family had recently moved to Abu Dhabi from the United States. I had never met anyone from America before. To be honest, I wasn't expecting much. My uncle Mustapha says that most Americans eat unhealthy foods and watch TV all day. As he's my favourite uncle, I tend to believe what he says.

One day, the teacher put Jack next to me in class. During our lunchbreak, we sat together, and he told me that his favourite hobby is indoor wall climbing. "That's my all-time favourite thing too!" I said. Jack told me that his mother worries he might fall, and we laughed because mine does too. Jack told me that he hardly ever watches TV and prefers to read. I told him what my uncle said about Americans eating unhealthy food.

Jack said that some Americans do eat unhealthy food but that many don't. His family are vegetarian, and his mother hardly ever lets him eat sugar. I sympathised because mine is very strict about treats too. It turns out that Jack and I have a lot in common and we are now best friends.

These days when I meet new people, I try to judge them after I get to know them, not before.
💭

Where did the writer's prejudice about Americans come from? What changed it? Could you think of an example from your own life?

🎯 What is the main lesson from Jack's story?

🅐 Americans are very different from people in the UAE
🅑 You should always trust what your family tells you about other cultures
🅒 Get to know people as individuals before forming opinions about them
🅓 People from different countries always have a lot in common

⚠️Judging before getting to know someone

⚠️Believing gossip or second-hand opinions
⚠️Excluding or bullying based on appearance

💚Recognising that every person is unique

Class Discussion Questions
10 / 13
🎤
Teacher says

"Click each question to reveal ideas — but discuss FIRST! These questions have no single right answer."

😠
Why do people sometimes bully or exclude others who are different?
Often it comes from insecurity, fear, or wanting to feel superior. When we don't understand something, we can fear it — and that fear can turn into meanness. Prejudice is often a mask for our own insecurities about being different ourselves.
🧠
Is it possible to hold prejudices without realising it? How?
Yes — these are called unconscious biases. They can come from family (like Uncle Mustapha), media, social circles, or cultural narratives. We absorb these views without realising it. Awareness — noticing when we're making assumptions — is the first step to overcoming them.
📱
Can social media make prejudice better OR worse? How?
Both! Social media can help by exposing us to diverse cultures and stories. But it can also reinforce stereotypes through biased content, echo chambers (only seeing what we already believe), and online bullying that targets people for being different.
💪
What is the BEST way to overcome prejudice against a group you don't know?
Get to know individuals from that group — like the writer did with Jack. Read about their culture, eat their food, watch their films, listen to their music. Real knowledge built on genuine connection is the most powerful antidote to prejudice.
🏫
What can students do to challenge prejudice when they see it at school?
Speak up when you see bullying or exclusion. Include people who are being left out. Challenge stereotyped comments by asking "How do you know that?" Don't laugh at jokes that mock a culture or group. Be a positive role model — your attitude influences those around you.
